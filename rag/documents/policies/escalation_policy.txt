SOC ESCALATION POLICY

PURPOSE:
Define the conditions and process for escalating security incidents
between SOC tiers and to management/external parties.

TIER DEFINITIONS:
- Tier 1 (SOC Analyst): Initial alert triage, playbook execution,
  basic investigation, first response actions.
- Tier 2 (Senior Analyst / Incident Responder): Deep investigation,
  malware analysis, complex containment, forensic collection.
- Tier 3 (Lead / Threat Hunter): Advanced threat hunting, APT response,
  Active Directory / infrastructure compromise, strategic decisions.
- Management (CISO / Management): Business decisions, public disclosure,
  regulatory notification, ransom decisions.
- External: Law enforcement, CERT/CC, legal counsel, PR team.

ESCALATION CONDITIONS:

Tier 1 → Tier 2:
- Confirmed malware execution (not just detection).
- Successful brute force or credential theft confirmed.
- More than 3 hosts affected by a single incident.
- Lateral movement detected.
- Web shell confirmed active.
- Analyst unable to contain incident within 30 minutes.

Tier 2 → Tier 3:
- Domain Controller or Active Directory compromise suspected.
- 5+ hosts involved in a single incident.
- Ransomware spreading across the network.
- Advanced persistent threat (APT) indicators.
- Incident duration exceeds 4 hours without containment.

Tier 3 → Management (CISO):
- Ransomware confirmed with significant business impact.
- Data exfiltration of PII, PHI, or regulated data confirmed.
- Critical infrastructure system compromised.
- Media or regulatory enquiry likely.

Escalation to External:
- Law enforcement: when criminal activity (ransomware payment,
  data theft, insider crime) is confirmed. Required by some regulations.
- Legal counsel: when litigation risk, regulatory notification, or
  contract breach is possible.
- CERT: when nation-state threat actor is suspected.

ESCALATION TIMEOUTS:
- Tier 1 must escalate within 30 minutes if unable to resolve CRITICAL.
- Tier 2 must escalate to Tier 3 within 2 hours for unresolved HIGH/CRITICAL.
- All CRITICAL incidents must have a management notification within 1 hour
  of confirmation.

ESCALATION COMMUNICATION:
When escalating, provide:
1. Incident ID and current severity.
2. Affected assets and users.
3. Timeline of events (first seen, current status).
4. Actions taken so far.
5. Reason for escalation.
6. Recommended next steps.
