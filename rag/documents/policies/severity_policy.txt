SOC SEVERITY POLICY

LOW:
1-5 failed authentication attempts.
Informational log entries, routine policy violations.
No immediate threat indicators present.
SLA: Review within 24 hours.

MEDIUM:
6-20 failed authentication attempts.
Single-source port scan detected.
Suspicious process execution (moderate confidence).
Known bad IP contacted but no payload delivered.
SLA: Review within 4 hours.

HIGH:
More than 20 failed authentication attempts within 5 minutes.
Successful authentication after repeated failures from same source.
Malware detected (quarantined successfully).
Web application attack (SQLi, XSS) with indicators of exploitation.
Privilege escalation attempt detected.
SLA: Review within 1 hour.

CRITICAL:
Successful authentication after brute-force activity,
evidence of account compromise, or privilege escalation.
Active ransomware encryption detected.
Active data exfiltration in progress.
Lateral movement confirmed between internal hosts.
Domain Controller or Active Directory compromise suspected.
Web shell confirmed active.
SLA: Immediate response (within 15 minutes).

For SSH brute-force detection:
More than 20 failed authentication attempts from the
same source IP targeting the same account within 5 minutes
should be considered HIGH severity.

For RDP brute-force detection:
Treat the same thresholds as SSH. If RDP is exposed directly
to the internet (not via VPN), elevate severity by one level.

The absence of a successful login does not automatically
reduce the severity of a large-scale brute-force attempt.

Severity may be escalated based on asset criticality:
- Crown jewel assets (AD DC, financial systems, PII databases):
  elevate all alerts by one severity level.
- DMZ/internet-facing assets: elevate HIGH to CRITICAL if
  exploitation indicators are present.
